Best Landing Page Templates for SaaS in 2026: Complete Comparison

Your landing page is your best salesperson. It works 24/7, never takes a sick day, and makes a first impression in under 3 seconds. For SaaS companies, a high-converting landing page is the difference between growth and stagnation.

What Makes a Great SaaS Landing Page Template?

Before diving into specific platforms, here's what we evaluated:

  1. Conversion elements — Does it include proven SaaS conversion patterns (social proof, CTAs, feature sections)?
  2. Design quality — Does it look modern and professional, or dated and generic?
  3. Customization — How easy is it to change colors, fonts, layout, and content?
  4. Performance — Does it load fast? Is the code clean and optimized?
  5. Responsiveness — Does it work perfectly on mobile, tablet, and desktop?
  6. SEO foundation — Is the HTML semantic? Are heading tags structured correctly?
  7. Integration — Does it work with your analytics, email, and CRM tools?
  8. Support — Is there documentation and support when you get stuck?

Webflow Templates — Best for Custom Design

Rating: 9.2/10

Pricing: $14-49 one-time (templates), $14-39/month (hosting)

Technology: HTML/CSS/JS (Webflow visual editor)

Skill Level: Intermediate

Browse Webflow Templates →

Webflow templates are the gold standard for SaaS landing pages that need to stand out. The visual editor gives you complete control without writing code, and the template quality is consistently high.

What We Liked

  • Exceptional design quality. Webflow templates look custom-designed, not template-y. The best ones rival agencies charging $10k+.
  • Visual editor. Change anything — layout, colors, animations, interactions — without touching code. What you see is what you get.
  • Clean code. Webflow generates semantic HTML, optimized CSS, and fast-loading pages. Great for SEO.
  • CMS integration. Built-in CMS for blog posts, testimonials, case studies, and dynamic content.
  • Animations and interactions. Add scroll-triggered animations, hover effects, and micro-interactions easily.
  • Responsive by default. Every template is mobile-optimized, and you can fine-tune breakpoints.

What Could Be Better

  • Learning curve. The visual editor is powerful but not intuitive. Expect 2-3 hours to learn the basics.
  • Hosting cost. Webflow hosting is $14-39/month, more expensive than static hosting.
  • Limited to Webflow. You can't export the code and host elsewhere (unless you pay for the code export add-on).
  • Template cost adds up. Premium templates are $39-49 each, and you might want multiple for A/B testing.

Best For

SaaS companies that want a unique, high-quality landing page without hiring a designer. Best when you have 4-8 hours to invest in learning the tool.

Framer Templates — Best for Fast Iteration

Rating: 9.0/10

Pricing: $0-79 one-time (templates), $0-30/month (hosting)

Technology: React (Framer visual editor)

Skill Level: Beginner-Intermediate

Browse Framer Templates →

Framer is the fastest way to go from idea to live landing page. The templates are modern, the editor is intuitive, and you can publish in hours, not days.

What We Liked

  • Incredibly fast. We built and published a landing page in under 2 hours. The editor is drag-and-drop with live preview.
  • Modern designs. Framer templates have a clean, modern aesthetic that works well for SaaS. Lots of whitespace, good typography.
  • React-based. Under the hood it's React, so you can add custom components if needed.
  • Free tier available. You can start for free and upgrade when you're ready. Hosting starts at $0/month for simple sites.
  • Easy A/B testing. Duplicate pages, make variations, and test different approaches quickly.
  • Good animations. Built-in animation tools for scroll effects, transitions, and micro-interactions.

What Could Be Better

  • Less design flexibility than Webflow. Framer is faster but less powerful. Complex layouts can be harder to achieve.
  • Smaller template library. Fewer templates to choose from compared to Webflow or TemplateMonster.
  • Limited CMS. The built-in CMS is basic. For complex content needs, you'll need external tools.
  • React knowledge helps. While you don't need to code, understanding React concepts makes customization easier.

Best For

SaaS companies that need to launch fast and iterate quickly. Especially good for early-stage startups testing messaging and positioning.

TemplateMonster — Best Budget Option

Rating: 8.0/10

Pricing: $29-79 one-time (templates), hosting separate

Technology: HTML/CSS/JS, WordPress, React

Skill Level: Intermediate

Browse TemplateMonster →

TemplateMonster has been around for decades and offers a massive library of templates at reasonable prices. It's not the most modern platform, but it's reliable and affordable.

What We Liked

  • Huge selection. Over 500 SaaS landing page templates across different styles and technologies.
  • Affordable. Most templates are $29-79 one-time. No recurring fees for the template itself.
  • Multiple technologies. Choose HTML, WordPress, React, or other frameworks based on your stack.
  • Lifetime access. Buy once, use forever. No subscription required.
  • Support included. 6 months of support with most templates.

What Could Be Better

  • Design quality varies. Some templates look great; others look dated. You need to browse carefully.
  • Code quality inconsistent. Some templates have bloated code or outdated dependencies. Check reviews.
  • No visual editor. You're editing code or using WordPress, not a drag-and-drop builder.
  • Hosting is separate. You need to find and pay for hosting separately.

Best For

Budget-conscious SaaS companies that don't mind editing code or using WordPress. Good when you want a one-time purchase with no recurring template fees.

Themeforest — Best for WordPress Users

Rating: 7.8/10

Pricing: $39-69 one-time (templates), hosting separate

Technology: WordPress

Skill Level: Beginner-Intermediate

Browse Themeforest →

Themeforest (part of Envato Market) is the largest marketplace for WordPress themes. If you're already using WordPress, it's a natural choice.

What We Liked

  • Massive WordPress library. Thousands of SaaS-focused WordPress themes.
  • WordPress ecosystem. Access to 50,000+ plugins for forms, analytics, email, and more.
  • Visual page builders. Most themes include Elementor, WPBakery, or similar drag-and-drop builders.
  • Affordable. Most themes are $39-69 one-time.
  • Regular updates. Popular themes are updated regularly for security and compatibility.

What Could Be Better

  • WordPress overhead. WordPress can be slow and bloated if not optimized. Requires maintenance.
  • Security concerns. WordPress is a frequent target for attacks. You need to keep it updated.
  • Design quality varies. Like TemplateMonster, quality is inconsistent. Read reviews carefully.
  • Plugin dependencies. Many themes require specific plugins, adding complexity and potential conflicts.

Best For

SaaS companies already using WordPress or those who want the flexibility of the WordPress ecosystem. Good when you need a lot of functionality beyond a landing page.

Next Steps

  1. Define your requirements. Do you need a visual editor, or are you comfortable editing code? How unique does your design need to be?
  2. Set a budget. Remember to include hosting costs, not just the template price.
  3. Browse 3-5 templates. Don't just pick the first one you see. Compare layouts, features, and design quality.
  4. Check the reviews. Look for templates with good reviews and active support.
  5. Plan for customization. Budget 2-8 hours for customization, depending on the platform and your design requirements.
  6. Test on mobile. Over 50% of your visitors will be on mobile. Make sure your landing page works perfectly on all devices.
  7. Add analytics. Install Google Analytics or a similar tool before you launch. You need to track conversions.

Your landing page is your most important marketing asset. A good template gives you a strong foundation. The right platform makes customization easy. Choose wisely, and you'll have a high-converting landing page live in days, not months.
